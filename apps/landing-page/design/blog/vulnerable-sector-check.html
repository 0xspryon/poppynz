What a Vulnerable Sector Check actually checks, and what it doesn’t

Every Poppynz helper has one. Here’s how it differs from a standard police check, why it takes longer, and how to read one if a helper shares it with you.

If you’ve ever hired anyone to look after your kids, you’ve probably been told they have a “police check”. That phrase covers three very different documents. The one that matters most for childcare, and the one every Poppynz helper must hold, is the Vulnerable Sector Check, or VSC.

What a VSC is

A VSC is the deepest of the three levels of record check issued by Canadian police services. It exists for one situation: a person taking a position of trust or authority over children or vulnerable adults. Helpers can’t simply request one for themselves; the organisation asking for it, in this case Poppynz through our partner Credibled, must confirm the role qualifies.

It’s built on top of the two lighter checks, so everything they cover, it covers too. The difference is what gets added on top.

The three levels, side by side

LEVEL 1

Criminal Record Check

Convictions on the national repository. Same-day, name-based.

Not enough for childcare
LEVEL 2

Criminal Record & Judicial Matters

Adds outstanding charges, warrants, peace bonds and probation.

Required by Poppynz
LEVEL 3

Vulnerable Sector Check

Adds pardoned sexual offences and local non-conviction records.

Required by Poppynz

The first two are what most people mean by “police check”. They confirm whether someone has criminal convictions on the national database. Only the VSC goes further and asks the local police to search for pardoned sexual offences and non-conviction information they consider relevant to the role.

What it checks, and what it doesn’t

Included

  • Criminal convictions on the national repository
  • Outstanding charges and warrants
  • Judicial orders such as peace bonds and probation
  • Pardoned sexual offences (record suspensions)
  • Local police non-conviction information relevant to the role

Not included

  • Records from outside Canada
  • Driving history or provincial offences
  • Credit history or employment references
  • Youth records, in most circumstances
  • Anything that happens after the check is issued

That last point is the one families forget. A VSC is a snapshot. It says nothing about what a person did last week. This is why we ask helpers to renew theirs every two years, and why the Vetted badge on a profile shows the date it was last reviewed.

A clear check is the start of trust, not the end of it. It tells you what the police know. It doesn’t tell you who someone is.

Why it takes longer

A basic check can come back in minutes. A VSC routinely takes one to four weeks, and here’s why: if the applicant’s name and date of birth match anyone with a pardoned sexual offence, the police must confirm identity with fingerprints before the result is released. Common names get caught in this net constantly. It isn’t a red flag, just a slow one.

Two practical consequences for you as a family. First, a helper who says “my VSC is still in progress” is telling the truth more often than not. Second, on Poppynz, helpers don’t appear in search until the result is in and a person on our team has read it, so what you see in the app is already past this step.

How to read one if a helper shares it

Helpers aren’t obliged to show you the document, but many do. A few things to look for:

  1. The issuing service. It should be a Canadian police service, and the address on it should be where the helper lives.
  2. The level. The heading will say “Vulnerable Sector” explicitly. If it just says “Criminal Record Check”, it’s the lighter level.
  3. The date. Anything older than two years is out of date for Poppynz purposes.
  4. The result. It’s usually a single line: “No record”, or a list. If there is a list, that’s a conversation, not automatically a no.

A record check is personal information under PIPEDA. If a helper shares theirs, don’t photograph it, forward it, or store it. Look, and hand it back.

What Poppynz does with it

Every helper submits government photo ID, an enhanced criminal record check and a VSC before their profile goes live. The checks are fetched through Credibled and read by a member of our team, not an algorithm. If anything on the result needs a second look, we talk to the helper before deciding. Families never see the document itself, only the Vetted badge and its review date.
